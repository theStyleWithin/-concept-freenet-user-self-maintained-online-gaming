Concept Overview

This repository is an acting host for outlining a concept: using https://github.com/freenet as an alternative, user-self-maintained online gaming network — aiming to be the best free alternative to projects like Nextendo, Pretendo, and others in that space.

The Problem

Modern gaming is dominated by overpowered, unaccountable corporations that abuse the legal system and restrict access to goods that should, in practice, belong to the consumer once purchased. This shows up in a few ways:

Arbitrary agreements — licenses, terms of service, and privacy policies that no one actually reads when all they want to do is play a game.
Unaccountable enforcement — fair-to-unfair bans, timeouts, and social media suspensions handed down with little recourse.
Forced ecosystem lock-in — gaming platforms increasingly link to social platforms (Discord, X/Twitter) and actively encourage syncing your gaming account to them. Meanwhile, alternatives like Kick, Rumble, or Odysee are conspicuously left out — not because they're technically harder to integrate, but because the big ecosystems have no incentive to make room for platforms they don't control.
The Vision

An ultra-free gaming network that removes forced ecosystems from the equation entirely. Every player becomes a self-maintainer of their own gaming experience — but nothing is mandatory:

Game online in isolation, with or without voice chat.
No requirement to be on PlayStation Plus, Nintendo Switch Online, Xbox Live, or Discord voice specifically just to talk while you play.
Take your gaming group wherever you already are — X/Twitter, Discord, Telegram, Facebook, WhatsApp, Kick, Twitch, YouTube, Odysee, Rumble, Fluxer, Kloak, TeamSpeak, or anywhere else. The platform has no opinion on where you socialize.
Ignore the "sync your account" prompt entirely — the incumbent ecosystems aren't interested in supporting more user choice, so this network won't ask you to funnel through them at all.
Stretch Idea: Browser-Based Emulation

One direction worth exploring: forking existing emulators and embedding them as apps that run directly on the Freenet web, with users supplying their own privately and legally owned ROMs. Since Freenet is censorship-resistant by design, and these emulators would run on the network using each user's own hardware for processing/graphics, they'd be much harder to take down — and could stay available indefinitely.

These are the concepts as they stand for now.
